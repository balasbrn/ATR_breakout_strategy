ATR_breakout_strategy

ATR-compressed 20-bar high/low breakout · version v1

After a quiet stretch, does a strong 15-minute close beyond the last 20 bars' extreme keep going far enough to pay for itself? Tested first before costs (is there any edge at all?), then after costs (is it still profitable?), on each market separately.

§1The idea, drawn

Prior 20 bars (quiet)Breakout bar tStop = 1RSchematic, not data

Hypothesis. After volatility compression, a close beyond the prior 20-bar extreme with a strong impulse produces positive net R over the next 20 bars.

Prediction, not assumption: positive drift after the breakout.

Also measured: the share of trades that cross an overnight boundary on cash markets.

1R is the distance from entry to the stop. A result of +0.10 R means the average trade made a tenth of what it risked.

Measured per spoke, never pooled.

§3–5The rules, frozen

Bars and ATR
15-min bars from validated 1-min data
ATR(14), Wilder smoothing
ATR_pct = ATR / Close

Ranked as a percentage so US splits (unadjusted data) don't create jumps.

Compression (t−1)
ATR_Rank_30d[t-1] <= 20

Volatility is in the quietest fifth of the last 30 calendar days.

Breakout (t)
Long:  Close[t] > max(High[t-20:t-1])
       Close−Open >= 1.2 × ATR[t-1]
Short: Close[t] < min(Low[t-20:t-1])
       Open−Close >= 1.2 × ATR[t-1]

A close beyond the range, on a strong bar.

Entry, stop, 1R
Entry  Open[t+1]
Long   stop Low[t],  1R = Open[t+1] − Low[t]
Short  stop High[t], 1R = High[t] − Open[t+1]

One open position per symbol; a signal on a busy symbol is skipped (skipped_busy).

Gap rule
Long:  Open[t+1] <= Low[t]  → cancel
Short: Open[t+1] >= High[t] → cancel

Counted as skipped_gap, excluded from R.

Bar 5 and exit
At Close[t+5]: not in profit → exit
              in profit → stop to entry + cost
Any remaining → exit at Close[t+20]

Stops checked intrabar; a hit exits at the stop level.

Fixed parameters, chosen by reasoning before any data: 20 · 30 · 20 · 1.2 · 14 · 5 · 20 bars. No volume, funding, spread or order-book inputs.

§6 · §10How the result is decided

    Order matters. Stage A (gross) results are written down before Stage B (net) is computed. If Stage A fails, Stage B is still reported, but the strategy is recorded as having no gross edge. Failing means: reported as failed, no re-tuning under v1.

    Cost in R: try it

    Costs are charged in price, but results are in R. A tight stop makes 1R small, so the same fee is a bigger share of it. Net R = gross R − cost ÷ 1R.

    Cost per trade in R

    Example arithmetic only, not a result. A gross edge smaller than this number disappears after costs. The cost-to-ATR filter is a Pass 2 item, not part of v1.

    Caveats recorded with the costs. The FX 1.5 bp card lists US at 2 bp while the journal gives 4.9 bp, so both base and stress are pre-registered. Crypto prices are spot but costs model perpetuals; basis is not modelled. Crypto base slippage (2 bp) is below the 13 bp measured, so base is the optimistic case and stress the realistic one. Funding is not modelled (≈ 0.7 bp per 5-hour hold on the Prop account). The pass rule uses the base case.

    RefsResearch behind this test

    Papers and books that shaped the idea and the test design. They inspire the rules; none of them tests this exact rule. No peer-reviewed study we found tests an intraday "volatility compression, then breakout" rule across these markets, which is the gap this test is meant to fill. This list is kept in a separate references file, so the frozen contract text stays unchanged.

    SourceTypeWhat it foundHow it shapes this test
    The breakout idea
    Brock, Lakonishok & LeBaron (1992), "Simple Technical Trading Rules and the Stochastic Properties of Stock Returns", Journal of Finance 47(5), 1731–1764Peer-reviewedOn the Dow Jones 1897–1986, buying when price breaks above its recent high (the "trading range break") was followed by higher returns than selling at a break below the low.The core rule: a close beyond the prior 20-bar high or low. Tested here on 15-minute bars instead of daily.
    Gao, Han, Li & Zhou (2018), "Market Intraday Momentum", Journal of Financial Economics 129(2), 394–414Peer-reviewedIntraday moves carry on within the day: the S&P 500's first half-hour return predicts its last half-hour.Support for the idea that a strong intraday move can continue over the next hours (20 bars ≈ 5 hours).
    Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz (2024), "A Profitable Day Trading Strategy for the U.S. Equity Market", Swiss Finance Institute Research Paper 24-98Working paperAn opening-range breakout on US stocks was profitable after costs, mainly on "stocks in play".     Why compression first
    Bollerslev (1986), "Generalized Autoregressive Conditional Heteroskedasticity", Journal of Econometrics 31(3), 307–327Peer-reviewedVolatility clusters and reverts to its average: quiet periods tend to be followed by more active ones.The reason to wait for compression (ATR in the lowest 20% of 30 days) before a breakout.

    Sullivan, Timmermann & White (1999), "Data-Snooping, Technical Trading Rule Performance, and the Bootstrap", Journal of Finance 54(5), 1647–1691Peer-reviewedRe-tested Brock et al. allowing for the many rules tried; the best rules lost their edge in later data.Why the rules are frozen in advance, out-of-sample is required, and the holdout runs once.
    Park & Irwin (2007), "What Do We Know About the Profitability of Technical Analysis?", Journal of Economic Surveys 21(4), 786–826Peer-reviewed surveyMany studies report profits, but many have data-snooping, cost or risk problems.Why costs, a random-entry benchmark and the best-symbol-removed check are part of the pass rule.

    §11What kills it, and known risks

    Fails rules 1–3

    No edge at these costs in this period. Reported as failed; no re-tuning under v1.

    Passes dev and OOS, fails holdout

    Recorded as "did not survive the holdout".

    Survivorship and look-ahead

    US list chosen for 2026 popularity; crypto list omits dead coins. Positive results may be inflated. Shown on every result.

    Costs dominate

    The 3× cost-to-ATR constraint is not in Pass 1; it becomes a Pass 2 filter only if a gross edge exists.

    Spot vs perpetual

    Crypto history is spot; costs model perpetuals. Basis and funding not modelled.

    Small crypto sample

    The completeness rule may leave 1–3 coins. Reported as low-power and never pooled.

    Crypto data rules (O5). Keep a coin only if ≥ 90% of minutes are present in every calendar year of its dev and holdout windows. Fill isolated gaps with a flat bar marked as filled, and report counts. No entry if bar t, any of t+1…t+5, or more than two of t−20…t−1 contain more than 3 filled minutes. A secondary row with crypto dev from 2019-01-01 is declared now and reported beside the primary, not used for pass/fail.
